# 快说 Kuài Shuō: the overnight plan

**The idea.** A TikTok-style vertical feed of tiny, ridiculous, high-stakes scenes in Mandarin. Each one lasts under
two minutes and stops at the moment that matters: you have to say the key phrase, out loud, before the clock runs
out. Your eSIM is dead and you need a stranger's phone; a tiger is gaining on you; your aunt is asking about your
salary at New Year. Stakes make phrases stick, the way you'd learn to run fast if a tiger were behind you.

**Who it's for.** Someone who can already hold a conversation but reads and writes little, and wants to get to
proficient in speaking, reading and writing. So the Chinese is natural, colloquial mainland Mandarin (HSK 3 to 5
chunks, not textbook lines), every line is shown in characters, pinyin is training wheels that come off, and you
type and draw characters as well as speak.

## Versions to compare in the morning

All five share one engine and one content format, so the best ideas move between them.

| | Version | One line | Trains most |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Scenes** (刷) | Cinematic feed cards: the story plays, the stage freezes, you say the line, the story reacts. | Speaking in context |
| B | **Chase** (追) | Arcade run: a tiger (or a goose, or your relatives) closes in; every right phrase buys distance. | Speed and automaticity |
| C | **Chats** (聊) | WeChat-style dramas: voice notes and texts arrive; you reply by voice note, keyboard or tiles. | Reading and writing |
| D | **Calls** (接) | An incoming call. No subtitles unless you peek. Stay silent too long and they hang up. | Listening under pressure |
| E | **Mix** (快说) | The feed people would actually use: all four card types, plus 10-second review and writing cards. | Everything, spaced |

## The engine (shared)

- **Voices**: every line pre-generated by a local open-source TTS model (Kokoro-82M v1.1-zh, Apache-2.0), packed one
  audio file per scene. No API credits spent. A slow version of every phrase you must say.
- **Listening to you**: the browser's own speech recogniser (`zh-CN`). Answers are judged by sound (pinyin, with
  tones forgiven but flagged), so a homophone the recogniser picked still counts, and "you said mài, not mǎi" can be
  pointed out. Each beat lists the words that must be there, so many phrasings pass.
- **No microphone?** (in public, or where the page can't get one) every beat also works by tiles (build the
  sentence from characters), by typing with the phone's Chinese keyboard, or by saying it aloud and checking yourself.
- **Reading**: characters always, pinyin above them per setting (always / new words only / off), tap any word for its
  pinyin and meaning (CC-CEDICT), English on demand.
- **Writing**: stroke-order drawing of key characters (Hanzi Writer data), typed replies in the chat version.
- **Memory**: phrases you meet come back as quick review cards on a spaced schedule (kept on the phone only).

## Where it runs

- **Phone, quickest**: a private claude.ai artifact. That frame can't use the microphone, so speaking beats fall
  back to "say it aloud, then check"; everything else works.
- **Phone, with real speech recognition**: `node kuaishuo/tools/serve.mjs --lan` on the Mac, then the printed
  https address on the phone (same Wi-Fi, accept the self-signed certificate once). Desktop Chrome on localhost
  works too.

## Quality bar

- Every scene plays start to finish in a headless phone-sized browser, success and failure paths, with no console
  errors (`node tools/test.mjs`).
- Every voiced line is run back through a local speech recogniser and compared with its script; lines that come
  back wrong are rewritten or regenerated.
- The Chinese is checked by a separate editor pass: natural, correct, correctly toned pinyin, no gendered address
  forms aimed at you.
- Phone first: 360 to 430 px wide, safe areas, thumbs, one hand, sound off handled.
