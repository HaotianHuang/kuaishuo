# P2: the For You ranker and the simulator (contracts)

README.md §5 and algorithm.md §4 and §6 give the reasons and the numbers; this file wins for P2.

## rank.js (new, DOM-free: Node runs it in tools/sim.mjs)

```js
const r = createRanker({ data, learner, assist, srs, marks, store, now, rng, mode, runs, resume })  // everything injected; rng seeded in tests
r.next() → { item, why } | null  // the next card, chosen as it's needed (feed.js asks one ahead); null: nothing to deal now
r.again() → { item, why } | null // the card just dealt, picked again before it's on screen (null once it is, or when
                                 // nothing could take its place: then it stays)
r.after(row)               // every events.js row as it's logged, line and card: the session and the arms learn from them
r.back()                   // the page back from hidden: the time away isn't time spent (events.js's)
r.unsee(ids)               // phrases a run never reached: as due as before (mix.js's run leave(), as deal-v1's)
r.waiting()                // the reviews due that it deals below an end card (an end card's "Review the n due ↓")
r.session()                // what this visit has dealt and how it went (for why, the recap and tests)
```

- `learner` and `assist` are the modules (learner.js, assist.js); `store` is store.js `kv` (the arms live in `ks.arms`);
  `now` is time.js `now`; `rng` a function returning [0, 1) (Math.random in the app, seeded in tools).
- As built, three more: `mode(scn, kind)`, how the player will answer: for `kind` `'scn'` a way or `beat → way` (feed.js's
  `answering()`), for `'review'` and `'run'` the way those cards answer (typed where the phone can't listen, as cards.js
  and chase.js do); without it every line is predicted as say-and-check; it's asked once a pick for each format and kind
  (feed.js `answering()` reads Settings each time). `runs`: `chase.createRun` exists (default true). `resume()`: the
  visit under way when the ranker is made inside one, `{ start, rows }` (events.js's session and the rows logged since
  it began): mix.js passes it, so the Mix opened again (from the versions, a tab the phone reloaded) carries the visit
  on: its cards and lines rebuilt from the rows, its stories played, its rules, τ, fatigue and the recap's clock going on
  (the arms heard every row already: nothing is counted twice).
- **Re-picks:** `next()` asked again before the card it gave has come on screen picks that place again (mix.js's
  `again()` may call either). A card counts as on screen once one of its lines or its card row is heard, or the card
  before it has been left; the first card a ranker deals is on screen as it's dealt; a card dealt but never shown is
  picked again even once a visit has ended under it. A re-pick that chooses the same card returns the same item object,
  and while no card row has changed the arms it draws as the pick it replaces did, so a swap means the visit changed,
  not the dice.
- The ranker keeps the **session** itself, from its own picks and the rows it hears: the cards dealt (kind, format,
  topic, outcome, state, how it was left), the line results, the minutes, the last A. A session is events.js's: a
  visit, 30 minutes away ends it (time with the page hidden isn't time spent: `back()`). It's "returning" when this
  phone has played before (any line in the learner model or any srs entry). Back after half an hour with a card still
  up, the new visit starts with that card (its story played, its format the last), and the card after it is the new
  visit's to pick.

**item:** the Mix's kinds, as mix.js deals them today, plus new ones:
- `{ kind: 'scn', scn, lv, A, tau }`: `scn` tailored to the chosen say level (`content.tailor(scn, lv)`; the base level
  is the scenario itself), `A` the rung the ranker scored it at (`assist.startAt()`: forCard's own start, the five-card
  budget included), `tau` the τ that chose it. feed.js `assisted()` still chooses the rung as the card comes on screen
  (from where the last card got to), which is the one played; passed `tau` (`forCard(scn, { …, tau })`), it chooses
  the rung the ranker scored, else forCard's own τ 0.8 (the simulator runs both: `--tau=0.8`).
- `{ kind: 'review', p }`, `{ kind: 'run', ps, audio }`, `{ kind: 'write', item }`, `{ kind: 'shadow', item: { scn, step } }`,
  `{ kind: 'end' }`: as mix.js deals them.
- `{ kind: 'recap', said, nailed, written, back, next }` (new): the session's end on a high: lines said and nailed (ids,
  first time, y ≥ 0.9) this visit, words written, how many phrases come back by the end of tomorrow, the next episode
  (`{ id, ep, en }`: its id, number, and the last one's cliffhanger) or null. Swiping past it keeps going (never a
  wall); the next recap comes 8+ cards later.
- `probe` is P3's placement line: not in P2.

**why:** `{ score, E, F, V, N, tau, p, A, lv, rule }`. `#mix?why` shows it under each card. As built also `prop` (the share
of 16 Thompson draws the pick wins: the propensity, for off-policy evaluation), `n` (cards the rules allowed), `fat`
(fatigue) and `cls` (a story's: `new`, `level`, `rematch`). `rule`: `first`, `first*` (no story reached p̄ 0.85: the
surest), `score`, `due` (a review R3 required), `sure win`, `recap`, `end`, `below end`, and where nothing the rules
allow is left but stories are, `format*` (R2 gave way) and `surest*` (R6 gave way). mix.js logs `rule`, `p`, `A`, `tau`
and `prop` on each card's row (events.js), so For You can be weighed against v1 from the log.

### Candidates

- Unplayed scenarios, each at the say levels it has: the base, plus any `levels` overrides (`content.tailor()`). Each is paired with
  the assist `assist.choose()` gives it.
- A played scenario at a level whose lines are mostly new to you (the same moment, other lines). (As built.)
- Rematches: lost earlier, or liked 3+ days ago; **or with most of its lines due** (as built: spaced review in the story
  itself, at your rung, which a flash crisis hasn't). None of these in the visit that played them, and **only when no
  story new to you is allowed** (never played, or a level whose lines are mostly new): as first built they won on V
  (a line met yesterday in a 3- or 20-minute box is fully overdue, V 1.0 against a new story's 0.63) and were 45–57% of
  For You's stories, learners the model rates weak getting the same 5–8 stories every visit while most of the
  catalogue went undealt. Now 13–25%, nearly all once the stories in reach are used up. (What it costs: below, due
  reviews served.)
- Due reviews: a flash crisis, or a run when 3+ are due. As built: a run of the five longest overdue (flash crises instead
  right after a chase, R2); flash crises the ten longest overdue; a word due as a word to write again.
- Write cards from the last two scenarios; shadow cards (the last two scenarios' middle lines; mix.js `heard()` swaps in
  the first where the story wasn't seen through).
- The next episode of a series in progress (`series.ep`). As built, a series' episodes come in order (ep k once ep k−1 is
  done).
- A recap.

### Hard rules

- Card 1 of a session is a scenario with p̄ ≥ 0.85 and the top E.
- No format twice running.
- While anything is due, a review at least every 4th card, never two in a row.
- Reviews, writes and shadows together are ≤ 40% of a session.
- None of them in the first 3 cards.
- Nothing under p̄ 0.55, even at A5.

As built and held by tools/rules.mjs (rank.js's header): R1 card 1 as above; where no story reaches p̄ 0.85 at the rung
the ladder allows (a step from where the last card got to: A2 to A3 at most on a first visit), the surest story is dealt
and why says `first*` (counted apart, as forced). R2 a run is a chase; a review, a write, a shadow each a format of its
own. R3 "anything due" is a review the ranker may deal (its p̄ at least the floor, not dealt this visit at that due
time, and one R2 allows: a word to write again owed straight after a word written waits a card, where it used to leave
no card the rules allowed); a word to write again is a review. R4 holds after every card, and a write or shadow only where the review R3 may
need next would still fit. R6 is read as algorithm.md's "in reach": a story out of reach even at A5 is never dealt; one in
reach but not at the rung it would start at is the fit's to weigh (the ladder eases it as it goes); a card with no rung
(a review, a run, a word, a shadow) is held at its own p. Read at the scored rung, the floor shut out most of the
catalogue whenever the ladder lagged a step (a studied learner had nothing to play on day 2). **R7 (new): a call whose
listening is more than a level above what you can surely follow waits, through a phone's first 3 visits:
`listen ≤ 1 + level(θL − sd(g))`** (the done-when's "never" needs a rule; g's sd, not L's, since any line tells the model
about g while L is heard mostly on calls). As first built it held for good, and fed itself: L is heard mostly on calls,
so θL barely moved (a dialect learner after 82 lines at 85% right sat at 2.997 against the 3 a listen-3 call needs), and
over 14 days dialect, studied and new learners met 14, 1 and 0 calls in some 1,800 stories each (calls are a quarter
of the catalogue; v1 deals one every fourth story). The done-when asks the "never" of the first 3 visits; after them the
fit weighs a call (A4 and A5 never hang up, A5 has subtitles), and every persona meets calls (the simulator's format
table). Recaps and end cards are no format and none of the 40%. **With no card the rules allow while stories are left
for the visit, two rules give way, in order and said so** (why): R2 (`format*`: the best story in reach of the last
card's format: one format left), then R6 (`surest*`: none in reach even at A5, the surest story, another format first);
R7 and a series' order never do. As first built the ranker dealt its end card there, as a visit's first card too: a
learner the model rates weak (a silent one in two cards) got an end card every visit with 20 stories unplayed, and
mix.js handed such visits to v1's dealing, which broke R1, R2, R4, R5, R7 and series order (a listen-3 call to a new
learner in their first visit, Back Home episode 3 before 1 and 2). **Only with no story left does an end card close the
visit's stories, and below it the reviews due come, as many as the learner asks for, whatever their chance** (v1's: the
floor is for what the feed deals unasked; as first built, a learner the model rated weak saw "Review the n due ↓" and
nothing came).

### Score

score = 0.35·E + 0.30·F + 0.25·V + 0.10·N, plus 0.15 for a series in progress.

- **E:** Thompson draws from Beta arms for format and topic (`arms`, kept in ks.arms).
  - Priors from the critics' ranking: scene .78, call .76, chat .72, chase .70, weight 4. Goal topics start at Beta(4,1).
  - Updates from card rows: finished +1α; left before 30% +1β; left at 30–90% +0.5β; liked or replayed +1α; ×0.98 each session.
- **F:** exp(−½((p̄−τ)/0.1)²), where p̄ is the mean learner.predict over the card's lines at its A (as built: each line at
  its own rung, `assist.lineRung()`, so an L0 line's first meetings count with the help they get; a typed line by its
  sound, as `assist.choose()` judges it). A review is predicted as a line at A2: **algorithm.md's R·p is dropped.** With
  R = 2^(−Δt/(4·interval)) and the 3- and 20-minute first boxes, a phrase met yesterday reads as gone (2^−18), so under
  the floor no review came across visits at all (the simulator: 39 reviews and 11 runs to 463 stories for Mandarin-at-home
  learners, 1–8% of due phrases served within two visits); and any multiplicative R says a half-forgotten phrase is half
  as sayable as a new one, though the cue and the skill are still there. The line's own chance already carries how it
  went before (its difficulty moves with each answer). With it (and the rematches above): review ECE 0.21 → 0.06–0.17,
  due phrases served within two visits 1–8% → 45–69%. A write
  is predicted at the rung it's played at (the last card's: its leniency, a second trace at A5).
- **V:** for a review, 0.5 + 0.5·min(1, overdue/interval); for a new scenario, the share of new lines plus the skill gaps it exercises, weighted by goals; for write and shadow, their skills' gaps.
  As built: a new scenario 0.6·new-line share + 0.4·Σ gk·loadk (algorithm.md §4; goals all 1 until P3 asks for them); a
  rematch what its lines are worth, a review's worth each one due and 0.25 each one not (algorithm.md's flat 0.5 left a
  rematch whose lines were all due no likelier than one whose lines weren't); a run its phrases' mean. A gap counts only
  as far as the model is sure of it (1 − s/s⁰), so a skill never yet observed isn't chased: tones without a microphone,
  as meant, but also writing for anyone who speaks every line, so write cards are rare for them (P3's goals, or an
  exploration rule, should decide whether a heritage learner is asked to write early).
- **N:** variety: −0.5 for the same format as the last card, −0.25 for a format in the last 3, minus tag overlap.

### τ

| Situation | τ |
|---|---|
| Base | 0.80 |
| A returning session's first 2 cards | 0.90 |
| One card after a loss, or 2+ misses in 5 lines | 0.92 |
| After 3 strong cards ("Harder one?") | 0.70 |
| Tired (fatigue > 0.5) | 0.88 |

As built, in this order: after a loss (or 2+ misses) 0.92; a visit's first card, and a returning visit's first two, 0.90
(the first visit's first card too: R1 asks p̄ ≥ 0.85 of it, and chosen at 0.80 its rung would give 0.80 on average);
tired 0.88; after 3 strong cards 0.70; else 0.80.

### Fatigue and ending

Fatigue = 0.4·min(1, minutes/20) + 0.3·(thinking slowdown) + 0.3·(skips and time-outs in the last 5 cards).

Over 0.7, or after 12+ minutes on a win, end on a high: a sure win (p̄ ≥ 0.9, ≤ 60 s), then a recap.

As built: thinking slowdown is the median of the last 5 lines' time over their clock against the visit's median, less 1,
in [0, 1]; skips are cards left before their first line. No story runs 60 s (the shortest is 71 s), so the sure win is a
review, a run, a word or a shadow when the rules allow one. **The recap comes straight after a win only:** with no sure
win to deal after a card that didn't go well, the visit goes on (τ 0.92 aims the next card at a win) and the recap waits
for one. As first built a loss at fatigue 0.7 (one bad card 9 minutes in can cross it) went straight to the recap; and
a word written as the sure win never got its recap, its outcome unknown till its row came as it was left (a word
written to its end is now done as it ends: its stars go into spaced review then). rank-check holds every recap to a
win before it and every sure win won to its recap (1,584 of 1,584 and 104 of 104 in its stress picks).

### The wall

rank.js never imports `econ.js` or `ads.js` (P5). The same seed and history give the same picks with ads on or off.

As built: tools/rank-check.mjs walks rank.js's imports at every depth (content.js, files.js, picks.js, levels.js,
store.js, time.js: all DOM-free) and finds neither; and deals 100 picks over 4 days twice from one seed, then again with
`KS_AD`, `ks.econ`, `ks.ads` and ad rows in the log: the same picks each time.

## deal-v1.js (new): v1's dealing, kept

mix.js's `order()` and `more()` move here unchanged and DOM-free. `#mix?deal=v1` uses them, and the simulator measures
against them.

```js
const d = dealV1({ data, srs, marks, now, runs })  // runs: chase.createRun exists (a run card can be dealt)
d.more() → items      // up to 3, exactly as mix.js's more() deals them today
d.waiting()           // what's due or below (the end card's "Review the n due ↓")
d.unsee(ids)          // phrases a run never reached: as due as before (mix.js's run leave())
```

The pickers it needs (`linesToShadow`, `wordsToWrite`) are in `app/shared/picks.js`, DOM-free (cards.js re-exports them).

As built: tools/rank-check.mjs takes `order()` and the dealing inside `mount()` from mix.js at 3216e80 (P1, before the
ranker), as text from git, and deals it beside deal-v1.js over the same play (stars, wins and losses, runs left part-way,
minutes and days passing, the Mix reopened; with and without runs; data/ and p4b-2): the same 45,624 cards and the same
`waiting()` after every one. (`heard()`, which swaps a shadow's line at card time, stays in mix.js.)

## In the app

- mix.js plays through the ranker: `#mix` is For You, `#mix?deal=v1` is v1's dealing, and `#mix?why` (or
  `?deal=v1&why`) shows each pick's `why` under its card. A small overlay, for tests and the owner; nothing else
  changes on screen.
- **Lazy dealing:** feed.js asks for one card ahead, not three. A new `feed.replace(i, item)` swaps a card that hasn't
  come on screen yet. The Mix picks card i+1 as card i comes on screen (`onActive`), and again when card i is done
  (its outcome and the rung's move are known), swapping it if the choice changed.
- The recap card is new, in `app/protos/recap.js`: its lines nailed are right the first time with no help beyond the
  rung's (learner.js `outcome()` ≥ 0.9, as the ranker counts them; as first built it counted any line with three stars,
  which a wrong try first keeps: a dialect player wrong first on every line read "27 lines nailed" against the ranker's
  0), and with none it shows the lines said.
- Every row events.js logs goes to `r.after(row)` (events.on). Opened again inside a visit, the ranker is given the
  visit's rows (`resume`); the page back from hidden, `r.back()`. Each card's row in the log carries the ranker's
  `rule`, `p`, `A`, `tau` and `prop` (feed.js asks a card's controller for `row()` as it comes on screen). An end card or
  a recap is logged but not tallied as a card played (#stats counted the recap).
- `#mix?deal=v1`: deal-v1.js `next()`, its batches handed over one card at a time, each asked for when v1's feed asked
  (three cards ahead: as it opened, as the card coming on screen was the third from the end, with nothing left to
  show). As first built it asked only when its queue ran dry, a card ahead, so its batches read what was due later than
  v1's did and its reviews moved (11 of 14 places differed in one played visit); rank-check now holds next() to the
  cards v1's feed showed, card for card.
- A card built or thrown away while it isn't the one on screen doesn't touch the sound: a call's reset stopped every
  voice (audio.stopAll()), so a call dealt or swapped a card ahead cut the card on screen mid-line.

## tools/sim.mjs (new)

- **Learners:** five personas (Mandarin at home, dialect at home, studied, new, advanced), 200 each.
  - True skills: persona + N(0, 0.6²); 10% mislabelled.
  - True line difficulty: b⁰ + N(0, 0.5²).
- **Answers:** drawn from the true model. Self-ratings over-claim by 0.1 in half the runs. Learning: each line adds 0.01·w to the true skill; memory half-life doubles on a success.
- **Engagement**, under three assumptions (base, failure-averse, boredom-averse):
  - P(abandon card) = 0.05 + 0.4·max(0, 0.6 − p̄) + 0.2·[same format].
  - P(end session) = 0.04 + 0.25·[lost or 2+ misses] + 0.1·[p̄ > 0.97] + 0.01·minutes.
  - P(back tomorrow) = 0.55 + 0.15·[ended on a win] + 0.1·[8+ min] − 0.15·[frustrated].
- **Runs:** 14 simulated days, For You against deal-v1. Reports go to `review/sim/`.
  - Cards play through the assist ladder as in the app (assist.forCard, after), with the learner model learning.
  - **v1** in the table below is deal-v1 at A2 throughout (v1 as it shipped). Also report deal-v1 with the ladder
    (today's app after P1), so it's clear what the ranker itself adds.
  - Content: `data/` (what ships), and `--data=<dir>` for a gate build. `data/` has nothing below L1, so the "new"
    persona can't reach the success band there (assist-check shows the same); judge "new" on a build with L0
    (`review/gate/p4b-2/data`: 8 L0 stories, variants for ten v1 ones), and report both.

As built (`node tools/sim.mjs --data=<dir> --seed=1 --n=200 [--audit] [--tau=0.8]`, about a minute a build):
- The truth is tools/truth.mjs's, which tools/assist-check.mjs now plays by too (its output unchanged): every story card
  is played by the same player, with hooks for what the simulator adds. **advanced** is assist-check's fluent player
  (L 2.6 … W 1.0, answering Mandarin at home). Half the learners have a microphone (they speak), half don't (say and
  check; a chase, a chat's voice reply and every review typed). p4b-2 holds the 8 L0 stories, each with an L1 variant;
  the variants for ten v1 stories are p4b-1's, and p4b-5's the other ten's.
- **Memory** (the spec says only that the half-life doubles on a success): a line met before is right with
  R·σ(z + 1.2) + (1 − R)·σ(z), R = 2^(−t/h), h a day from the first meeting, doubled at each success: memory helps while
  it lasts and forgotten you're back to your skill, never below it. Lines said add 0.01·w to the true skills (w: the
  way's loadings, a call's shifted), as do words written and lines shadowed.
- **Engagement**, beside p2.md's: failure-averse doubles the failure terms (0.8·max(0, 0.6 − p̄), 0.5·[lost or 2+
  misses], −0.3·[frustrated]); boredom-averse doubles the boredom terms (0.4·[same format], 0.2·[p̄ > 0.95]) and leaves a
  story seen before 0.15 likelier. p̄ is the card's true chance at the rung it starts at; a miss is a line not right
  first time; frustrated is under half the visit's lines right, or 2 of its last 3 cards lost or left. At a recap the
  learner stops half the time, at an end card always; a won story is liked 12% of the time; lookups (a call's other
  lines by L, the rest by R, three chunks a story, fewer once the rung shows the English) go to the learner model as
  card rows. One visit a day (a new ranker each: a phone keeps nothing in memory overnight; the arms persist); a learner
  who doesn't come back is gone.
- **Paired:** the same true learner and seeds under every dealer, and common random numbers for the luck of a visit
  (leaving a card, a lookup, a like, stopping, coming back tomorrow are drawn by day and by the card's place, the same
  whichever dealer deals), so a dealer that makes a learner as likely to come back gets the same answer. Without them a
  dealer's luck wasn't the other's: Mandarin-at-home learners on p4b-2 read D7 × 0.95 against v1 though For You beat it
  on every day's chance of coming back (wins 0.88–0.93 against 0.72–0.86, 8-minute visits 0.64–0.74 against 0.41–0.53);
  with them, × 1.44.
- v1's dealers ask `more()` three cards ahead as the feed did; For You one ahead, picked as the card before comes on
  screen and again once it's done, as mix.js does. The rows go to the learner model, then the dealer, then the card's
  rung, as events.on hears them in the app, srs recording each line first (ask.js's order).
- The report also gives each dealer's stories by format (a rule that shuts a format out shows: R7 as first built held
  calls from dialect, studied and new learners for all 14 days), For You's share of rematches, where a rule gave way,
  and whether each recap came straight after a win; and placement over the learners the best estimator's bound covers
  (a true level not held at 0 or 5) beside the bound, so the two are compared on one population.
  `--audit` holds every pick to tools/rules.mjs, which now also checks what a pick must be where something else was
  owed (an end card only with no story left for the visit, first* and surest* the surest, a rule giving way only with
  nothing the rules allow, a rematch only with nothing new allowed), worked out from the stores, not from the ranker's
  own list. Mutations of rank.js that the checks now catch (the first two passed them before): an end card from the 7th
  card, first* the least sure story (the referee); rematches before new stories, card 1's equal-E ties left to the
  content file's order (the referee); E without its Thompson draws (rank-check's E); calls held for good (rank-check:
  every format to every persona over 14 days); an L0 chase's again encounter met under its own id (rank-check).

|---|---|

| Sessions ending on a win | ≥ 70% |
| D7 proxy (share of learners still playing on day 7) | ≥ v1 × 1.10 |
| True skill gain at day 14 | ≥ v1 |

- The full test matrix passes (and `--dist`), and the Mix plays through the ranker.
- `rank.js` and `deal-v1.js` import nothing with DOM code (Node runs them), and `rank.js` never imports econ.js or ads.js.
- Where a threshold turns out to be unreachable for any ranker on this content (as P1's per-skill target was), say so
  with the evidence, restate it here, and don't tune to a number that can't be met.

What lies behind the fails and the thin pass:
- **Placement** is P3's. The bound is now compared on its own learners, and the model falls well short of it there
  (studied learners' listening is placed within 0.6 for 3–9% of them against the bound's 50%; say 51–54% against 79%):
  so it isn't only that 12 lines carry too little information, as first restated here; the model's start is biased for
  some personas (a studied learner's listening most). P3's placement call, chat and ceiling line are the remedy; the
  report shows the gap per persona (its placement table).
- **Due reviews served** fell when rematches stopped crowding out new stories: they carried most of it (a whole story's
  lines reviewed at your rung), and now come only once nothing new is allowed (13–25% of stories on data/, 1–3% on
  p4b-2, 5–15% on p4b-3). What's left is flash crises and runs, about one card a short visit (R5: none in the first 3
  cards; R3: never two running), and none at all for learners the model rates weak, whose reviews are under the floor
  (they come only when asked, below an end card). Simulated, on data/ and p4b-2: one rematch a visit allowed ahead of
  new stories serves 28% and 24% worst (× 1.35 and × 1.25 D7, +0.026 and +0.037 gain, data/ ECE 0.054); runs of ten
  phrases, 20% (× 1.57). Which is wanted (or reviews dealt with a rung's help, so the floor lets them through) is a
  decision, not a fix; 85% isn't reachable here by any of them. By the simulator's own outcomes (D7, skill gain) new
  stories first does best.
